About the data

Sources. NYC Citywide Payroll Data (Fiscal Year), all 7,337,410 rows for FY2014–FY2026, downloaded November 12, 2026, with row counts matched to the city's totals. CCRB officer, allegation, complaint and penalty datasets for police misconduct. OATH Trials Division case status for employee-discipline cases. NYC Civil Service Titles for which titles require a background investigation before appointment. All from NYC Open Data.

Fired, then hired. OATH cases in which a judge recommended termination were matched to payroll by name at the agency that brought the charges, then checked for a later job at a different agency. Only names that pass the strict matching rules are counted. These are leads until confirmed against the published decisions.

Warning signs. "Paid less than half their salary" means a salaried employee whose regular pay for a fiscal year was under half their base salary, or who was listed on leave, in the two years before leaving (excluding their first year). Unpaid suspensions look like this, but so do medical and family leave.

What counts as a move. A salaried job marked ceased in a fiscal year, with the person never active at that agency again, followed by the same person active at a different agency in that year or later, with no overlap between the two jobs. DOE's separate payrolls are combined. Poll workers, per-session pay and agency reorganizations (Homeless Services and HRA, the Mayor's Office and its contracting office, ACS child care moving to DOE) are excluded.

What "ceased" means. Only that the person was off that payroll on June 30. Firings, resignations and retirements look the same. Leaving during probation is the closest public signal of "not asked back," but some probation exits are voluntary.

Probation. Estimated from start date and year of departure. Two years for police officers, correction officers, firefighters, EMTs and sanitation workers; four years for teachers; one year for everyone else, including civilian police staff such as school safety agents and 911 dispatchers.

Names. The public payroll has no employee ID, so people are matched on first name, middle initial and last name. Common names that can't be told apart are excluded.
